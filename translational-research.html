Scientific ownership

From biological question to interpretable output.

At NCI, my work on rhabdomyosarcoma combines single-cell analysis, recurrent cell-state discovery, regulatory interpretation, and projection into clinically annotated bulk cohorts. I connect study design and implementation with statistical evaluation and biological interpretation. This work produces an analytical basis for investigating tumor heterogeneity and selecting hypotheses for follow-up.

A complementary pan-pediatric project combines gene-regulatory networks, functional-dependency data, and pharmacologic resources to prioritize candidate mechanisms. SigClust addresses another practical need: statistically supported signature annotation of existing single-cell clusters. Together, these projects demonstrate how I turn a broad research question into explicit comparisons, reusable analyses, and evidence collaborators can examine.

Patient benefit is the research motivation.

Understanding tumor states and regulatory dependencies can help focus scarce experimental resources on better-supported questions. My contribution is to strengthen the evidence behind those choices, not to promise that a computational association predicts treatment benefit. Current pediatric-cancer projects remain in progress; nominated targets and exploratory outcome associations require independent and experimental validation.

Responsible, reviewable analysis.

My working principles are authorized data access, use of only the information needed for the analysis, documented provenance, and human review of conclusions. Public portfolio materials do not include patient-level data. I aim to make assumptions, limitations, and the next validation step visible in every handoff.

Two settings, one scientific standard.

I can contribute as a hands-on computational leader within a company or institute, or develop an independent academic group. In either setting, the goal is rigorous, reusable analysis that helps a team learn what to test next.
