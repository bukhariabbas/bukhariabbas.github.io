Translational research

From molecular patterns to testable hypotheses

My current pediatric oncology work connects molecular variation to three questions: which cell programs recur, which regulators may sustain them, and whether those programs carry information about patient outcomes. Each question requires a different comparison and a clearly defined next test.

Resolve tumor heterogeneity

Single-cell analyses separate malignant programs from immune and stromal signals. Non-negative matrix factorization identifies recurrent expression programs, while regulon analysis provides a complementary view of candidate transcriptional regulation. Explore the workflow and anonymized research examples.

Prioritize regulatory hypotheses

Consensus gene-regulatory networks, existing functional-dependency screens, and pharmacologic resources help assess candidate regulators. The output records supporting and conflicting evidence, model relevance, and the experimental comparison needed to test the hypothesis. Regulatory-dependency research.

Evaluate patient relevance

Projecting cell programs into clinically annotated cohorts allows outcome associations to be tested beyond the discovery cells. Molecular subtypes, clinical covariates, the number of events, and independent evaluation determine how much confidence an association warrants. Prognostic association alone does not establish treatment benefit.

Make the evidence reviewable

Reproducible R, Python, and Bash workflows connect inputs, parameters, results, and interpretation. SigClust applies the same principle to single-cell annotation by returning enrichment evidence alongside labels.

The pediatric cancer projects remain in progress. Their present outputs are research hypotheses and analyses for follow-up; experimental and clinical validation are subsequent steps.
