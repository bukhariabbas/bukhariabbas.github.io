Regulatory dependencies in pediatric tumors

Which regulators merit experimental testing?

Pediatric tumors can share expression patterns while depending on different regulatory programs. This project asks which transcription factors and target-gene sets are associated with a tumor lineage, and whether independent functional evidence supports investigating them.

As computational lead contributor, I built consensus gene-regulatory networks using ARACNe-AP and GENIE3, integrated tumor-specific expression and regulon evidence, and combined those results with functional-dependency and pharmacologic resources.

Combining distinct types of evidence

  1. Expression and networks: identify lineage-associated regulators and predicted target relationships.
  2. Functional dependencies: use existing DepMap screens to assess gene requirements in relevant models.
  3. Pharmacologic resources: evaluate supporting information from PRISM, ChEMBL, DrugBank, DrugCentral, and MIPE.
  4. Model comparisons: examine generic essentiality, proliferation, tissue composition, and contradictory evidence.

Each evidence source answers a different question. Network inference nominates relationships; dependency screens test requirements in particular models; drug annotations describe pharmacologic information. Agreement can strengthen a hypothesis without establishing a selective treatment.

Research output

The output is a set of candidate regulators with traceable evidence and model-specific questions for follow-up. The project analyzes existing dependency screens; experimental and clinical validation remain subsequent steps.

This complements the tumor heterogeneity analysis, which identifies recurrent cell programs and evaluates their association with patient outcomes.
