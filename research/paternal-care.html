Nature Communications · 2019 · First author

Parental care in stickleback fish

The question

Does becoming a parent produce a persistent transcriptional shift, distinct responses at each stage, or a cumulative progression? This study compared those alternatives across the paternal-care cycle in sticklebacks and examined their relationship to territorial aggression and parental responses in other species.

Reading the figure

Panel a shows stage-specific responses; panel b shows overlap between stages (larger circles indicate stronger statistical evidence); panel c shows cumulative responses. Red and blue indicate up- and down-regulation relative to controls.

Approach and findings

The analysis combined differential expression, explicit model comparisons, and cross-species analysis. It resolved distinct temporal patterns, connected some enduring responses in stickleback fathers with responses in mammalian mothers, and identified contrasting regulation associated with paternal care and aggression.

The public R scripts document edgeR analyses and permutation-based empirical false-discovery-rate calculations. Comparing alternative response patterns made the biological interpretation more specific than a single comparison between parents and nonparents.

My contribution

I contributed study design, performed the data analysis, and wrote the first manuscript draft. The work provides a framework for studying biological transitions: distinguish persistent changes from stage-specific responses, and evaluate conservation without assuming equivalent mechanisms across species.
