PLOS Genetics · 2017 · First author

The timing of neurogenomic responses

The biological question

A single endpoint can miss the sequence of molecular responses to an experience. This study asked how brain transcription and chromatin regulation change over time after a territorial interaction in male threespined sticklebacks.

Approach and findings

We combined time-resolved brain RNA-seq, H3K27ac chromatin evidence, and inferred regulatory relationships. Successive transcriptional responses involved different biological processes, including later immune and homeostatic components.

The analysis made timing part of the interpretation: an expression difference could reflect an early response, a later adjustment, or a persistent pattern. Complementary chromatin evidence helped formulate regulatory hypotheses.

These were bulk brain measurements. Immune-related expression did not identify the responsible cell population, and inferred regulatory relationships did not establish direct causation.

My contribution

As first author, I contributed conceptualization, data curation, formal analysis, methodology, software, visualization, and the original manuscript draft. This work established an approach to studying context-dependent regulation that continues in my cell-state analyses.
