Dynamic regulation in response to social experience

First-author research combining RNA-seq, H3K27ac chromatin evidence, and regulatory networks to understand molecular responses to a changing environment.

My role
First author · Analysis and methodology
Research output
Peer-reviewed multi-omic study
Status
PLOS Genetics · 2017

The biological question

How quickly can a social interaction reorganize molecular regulation in the brain? A single endpoint can miss the sequence of responses that follows a territorial challenge. This study asked how transcriptional dynamics and chromatin evidence jointly explain those changes in male threespined sticklebacks.

My contribution

As first author, I contributed to conceptualization, data curation, formal analysis, methodology, software, visualization, and the original manuscript draft. I integrated time-resolved expression measurements with H3K27ac chromatin evidence and candidate regulatory relationships.

Key findings

The study identified successive transcriptional responses associated with different biological functions and connected expression changes to chromatin variation and inferred regulatory networks. The result was a temporal, systems-level account of a behavioral response, rather than a single list of differentially expressed genes.

Analytical approach

The analysis combined temporal contrasts, functional interpretation, regulatory-network inference, and RNA-seq/chromatin integration. Distinguishing time-dependent responses from persistent patterns was central to the design. Inferred regulatory relationships remained hypotheses rather than proof of direct causation.

Why this work matters now

This project established my approach to multi-omic reasoning: study how coordinated regulation changes with context, compare alternative explanations, and use complementary assays to strengthen interpretation. I now apply the same principles to tumor cell-state plasticity and therapeutic hypotheses.
