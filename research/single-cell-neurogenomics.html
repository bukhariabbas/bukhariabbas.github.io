Honeybees: from genotype to phenotype

How do inherited genetic differences relate to cellular regulation and behavior? My honeybee work approaches this genotype-to-phenotype (G2P) question by connecting germline variation, brain gene expression, and cellular regulatory programs.

01 / Genotype

Germline variation

Genotype–expression associations, or eQTLs, connect inherited variants with expression differences. My public workflow prepares matched genotype and expression matrices and runs Matrix eQTL.

eQTL analysis code ↗

02 / Cellular regulation

Regulons & networks

A regulon links a transcription factor with its target genes. Single-cell network analysis places regulatory differences in specific brain cell types and regions.

03 / Phenotype

Behavioral differences

Soldiers and foragers provide contrasting behavioral phenotypes within colonies that differ in aggression.

The published result

Soldier–forager regulatory-network differences were greater in more aggressive colonies. This connects genetic and social context with cellular regulatory plasticity and collective behavior. Traniello et al., Nature Ecology & Evolution (2023).

The G2P framework combines complementary datasets. The paper evaluates network architecture and plasticity; it does not establish a direct causal variant-to-regulon-to-behavior chain in the same individual cells.

My contribution

I contributed methodology development and manuscript review and editing. My public genotype–expression analysis code documents the eQTL component.
