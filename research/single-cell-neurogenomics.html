Single-cell networks connecting genotype and behavior

A collaborative study connecting colony-level aggression, single-cell brain biology, and gene-regulatory architecture in honeybees.

My role
Coauthor · Methodology contributor
Research output
Cell-type-resolved regulatory interpretation
Status
Nature Ecology & Evolution · 2023

The biological question

How does genetic variation associated with colony-level aggression relate to regulation within individual brains? Cell-type and brain-region context offer a way to connect genetic architecture with coordinated molecular responses.

My contribution

I contributed methodology and manuscript review and editing as a coauthor during my IGB postdoctoral period at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. This was a collaborative single-cell and regulatory-network study, not a project I claim to have led independently.

Key findings

Regulatory differences between soldiers and foragers were more pronounced in aggressive colonies, with coordinated network effects in sensory and integrative brain regions. The study showed how cellular context can illuminate phenotype-associated regulation beyond gene-expression differences alone.

Analytical perspective

Single-cell transcriptomic profiles and gene-regulatory networks connect variation in phenotype to cell-type-specific regulation. The network framework helps ask where and how molecular differences are organized within a heterogeneous tissue.

Why this work matters now

This experience strengthened my ability to interpret complex traits through cellular networks. In cancer, the analogous challenge is to distinguish regulatory programs within malignant and microenvironmental populations and connect them to testable disease mechanisms.
