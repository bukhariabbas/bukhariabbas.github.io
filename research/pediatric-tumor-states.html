Methods in practice / Ongoing research

Parsing intratumoral heterogeneity

Intratumoral heterogeneity (ITH) is the variation among cells within the same tumor. Single-cell expression data help describe that variation as overlapping biological programs, then connect it to candidate regulators and patient-level questions.

These are complementary analyses. A cell can express several programs; an expression program does not automatically define a cell type or a genetic clone.

01 / Expression programs

Finding recurrent structure with NMF

Non-negative matrix factorization approximates an expression matrix as gene weights multiplied by program activities. Fitting NMF separately within samples, across several factor counts, helps distinguish recurring biological patterns from patterns specific to a single sample or model setting. Similar programs can be grouped into metaprograms.

For this approach, use non-batch-corrected expression for factorization, assess stability and sample coverage, and examine technical or housekeeping effects before interpreting a program. GeneNMF methods and implementation ↗

Read the pattern

The diagonal compares each program with itself. Off-diagonal blocks indicate programs with related gene-weight patterns. A strong block motivates a consensus program, but its stability, sample representation, and biological interpretation still require evaluation.

02 / Regulatory context

What might organize these programs?

The analysis keeps fusion-positive and fusion-negative comparisons separate, fits NMF to raw per-sample expression after excluding mitochondrial and ribosomal highly variable genes, and groups recurring programs by gene-weight similarity. Cell-level program scores can then be compared with potency, copy-number burden, and clinical strata, with sample-aware checks.

A regulon is a transcription factor and its associated target genes. It supplies a different perspective from NMF: target-gene activity can be used to infer regulatory state, while NMF describes coordinated expression without assigning a regulator.

Infer and score regulons

Network methods propose transcription-factor–target relationships. Motif-supported approaches such as SCENIC refine candidate target sets and score their activity across cells.

Connect regulons to programs

Test target enrichment in program genes and examine activity associations within relevant samples and cell compartments. Curated regulons can provide complementary evidence.

Regulon activity can be contrasted across the same unlabeled embedding and against per-cell NMF scores. Stability across samples, target-set specificity, and concordance with independent network methods matter before a factor is prioritized.

Target enrichment, regulon activity, and transcription-factor expression answer different questions. Agreement supports a regulatory hypothesis; it does not demonstrate that a factor controls the state. Perturbation or orthogonal evidence is needed to test that hypothesis. 03 / Patient outcomes

Do program scores track outcome?

Fixed gene programs can be scored in an outcome-annotated expression cohort, for example with GSVA. The inferential unit is the patient. In rhabdomyosarcoma, fusion-negative and fusion-positive analyses remain separate, with each group scored using programs derived from the matching subtype.

Patient-level transfer requires a fixed scoring rule, compatible gene coverage, and subtype-matched programs: fusion-negative patients are evaluated with fusion-negative programs, and fusion-positive patients with fusion-positive programs. Program scores can be examined continuously before any display split.

Kaplan–Meier curves describe survival for higher- and lower-score groups. Continuous-score Cox models address association and can adjust for available clinical factors. The score definition, endpoint, event coding, missingness, proportional-hazards assumption, and multiple-testing procedure all need to be explicit.

Read the curves

A downward step marks an event; censoring indicates follow-up without an observed event. The number at risk shows how much follow-up information remains. Late separation with few patients at risk is especially uncertain. Apparent separation alone is insufficient evidence of a reproducible prognostic marker.

What a defensible conclusion requires

Biological context

Bulk scores can reflect cell composition as well as cell-intrinsic activity. Sample effects, tissue processing, and correlated programs can also explain apparent associations.

Statistical context

Report effect sizes and confidence intervals, account for the tested family of programs, and assess event counts and model diagnostics. Small cohorts limit precision.

Independent evaluation

Keep program definitions fixed when moving to a separate cohort. Evaluate gene coverage and platform differences, and test whether the signal adds to clinical predictors.

Mechanistic follow-up

Use regulatory and functional evidence to design experiments. An outcome association alone does not establish causality or a treatment target.
