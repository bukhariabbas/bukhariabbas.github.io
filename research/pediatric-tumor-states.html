How the research framework guides the analysis

The atlas and circuit panels describe the analytical sequence: resolve cellular populations, discover recurring expression programs, then interpret candidate regulators. The niche and outcome panels connect this foundation to prospective spatial and cohort validation. The figure is a conceptual research framework; the results and scale described below are distinguished from those proposed extensions.

The biological question

Why do cells within a pediatric tumor occupy different transcriptional states, and which differences reflect meaningful biology rather than tissue composition or technical variation? I study rhabdomyosarcoma in relation to normal muscle development to identify reproducible programs and evaluate their potential clinical relevance.

What I own end to end

I lead analysis design, computational implementation, statistical evaluation, biological interpretation, and manuscript development. I integrated the RMS and muscle-reference atlas, developed quality-control and cell-classification workflows, and connected recurrent program discovery to regulatory interpretation and clinically annotated cohort analysis.

The full atlas contains 321,704 cells. After filtering, the RMS discovery set includes 104,917 malignant and microenvironmental cells from 21 patients. Cross-sample consensus analysis identified 32 metaprograms for functional and regulatory interpretation. This scale demonstrates the analytical scope; it does not establish predictive or clinical validity.

Methods connected to biological questions

I use Seurat and Harmony to integrate data; CopyKAT to inform malignant-cell classification; and CytoTRACE2 to estimate developmental potency. Multi-rank, per-sample NMF and cross-sample consensus distinguish recurring expression programs from sample-specific patterns. Pathway and transcription-factor analyses, including pySCENIC and DoRothEA, support regulatory interpretation.

I project fusion-matched programs into clinically annotated bulk cohorts using GSVA, then evaluate outcome associations with Cox regression, Kaplan–Meier analysis, proportional-hazards diagnostics, and multiple-testing correction. The purpose is to test whether a cell-state program carries reproducible patient-level information, not to assume that a visually persuasive survival curve establishes a biomarker.

Outputs that collaborators can inspect and extend

Deliverables include an integrated reference atlas, annotated programs, regulator-level interpretations, cohort-projection results, diagnostic summaries, and manuscript figures. Reproducible R/Python workflows on NIH Biowulf connect these outputs to documented inputs, parameters, and analytical decisions.

I also developed SigClust to make the relationship between signature scores and existing clusters explicit through enrichment testing and false-discovery-rate control. Together, these resources support candidate selection, critical review, and the design of subsequent validation studies.

Scope & integrity

The lead-author manuscript is in preparation. Current outcome associations are exploratory and do not survive false-discovery-rate correction; they are not validated prognostic biomarkers or treatment effects. GSVA scores measure expression enrichment rather than cell fractions. Developmental similarity does not establish a cell of origin, and spatial or experimental validation remains subsequent work.

Translational value

I connect a complex genomic analysis to explicit research decisions: which programs recur, which candidate regulators have convergent evidence, and what a follow-up cohort or experiment would need to show. This capability is relevant to senior computational roles in biotech, institutes, and translational research teams as well as to my proposed independent group.

Connection to my future group

My proposed independent program extends this reasoning across pediatric tumors: compare states, investigate regulatory dependencies, and evaluate tissue and clinical context. It will begin with public or appropriately authorized data and validation partnerships scoped to explicit biological questions. See the Future Group page for the research aims, scientific independence, and plans for training a computationally anchored team.

From tumor context to the brain microenvironment

My analysis of malignant, immune, and stromal programs develops an approach that is also useful for studying heterogeneous brain tissue: distinguish cellular abundance from within-cell regulation, identify recurrent programs, and test their relevance across cohorts. Spatial localization and inferred cell-cell relationships require additional evidence beyond transcriptomic association.

My proposed brain-genomics collaborations would combine this experience with my earlier behavioral and single-cell neurogenomics background. The aim would be to investigate neuronal, glial, and vascular states in their own biological context, not transfer tumor labels or presumed mechanisms to neurological disease.
