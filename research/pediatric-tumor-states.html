Current research

Pediatric tumor states and clinical heterogeneity

An integrated computational program connecting single-cell biology, gene regulation, and patient-cohort analysis in rhabdomyosarcoma.

My role
Computational lead
Research output
Interpretable programs and reproducible analyses
Status
Manuscript in preparation

The biological question

Why do cells within a pediatric tumor occupy different transcriptional states, and which differences reflect meaningful biology rather than tissue composition or technical variation? I study rhabdomyosarcoma in relation to normal muscle development to identify reproducible programs and evaluate their potential clinical relevance.

My contribution

I lead analysis design, computational implementation, statistical evaluation, biological interpretation, and manuscript development. I connect reference construction and copy-number-informed classification with developmental-state estimates, recurrent expression-program discovery, regulatory interpretation, and projection into clinically annotated bulk cohorts.

Analysis design

The workflow combines Seurat and Harmony for single-cell integration, CopyKAT for copy-number-informed classification, CytoTRACE2 for developmental-potency estimates, and multi-rank non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) for expression programs. Consensus across samples helps distinguish recurrent programs from sample-specific patterns. Transcription-factor and network analyses provide candidate regulatory explanations; GSVA projection and survival-model diagnostics test cohort-level associations.

Research outputs

The work produces annotated cell-state programs, documented quality-control decisions, reproducible R/Python workflows on NIH Biowulf, and cross-cohort comparisons. These outputs support biological interpretation and identify hypotheses that merit further validation.

Interpretation and next steps

Current outcome findings remain exploratory and do not establish validated prognostic biomarkers or treatment benefit. Program expression is not interchangeable with cell abundance, and resemblance to development is not proof of a tumor’s historical cell of origin. Independent cohorts and appropriately designed experimental or spatial studies are the next steps.

Relevance to therapeutic discovery

The transferable capability is to lead a complex analysis from a biological question to interpretable evidence: identify reliable disease states, evaluate alternative explanations, and specify what a follow-up experiment or cohort must demonstrate.
