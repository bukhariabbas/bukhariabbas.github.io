PLOS Computational Biology · 2013 · First author

Protein-fold evolution across genomes

The question

How did protein architectures diversify across evolution? This study used a census of structural domains across 492 genomes to examine the distribution and inferred history of protein-fold designs.

Approach and findings

We converted CATH structural-domain annotations into abundance matrices and reconstructed comparative phylogenies across classification levels. This linked a consistent representation of protein structures to comparisons among proteomes.

Under the study's evolutionary model, simpler architectures appeared earlier and were broadly distributed, while several more elaborate designs were associated with later specialization. The inferred ordering depends on the evolutionary model and the underlying structural annotations.

The analysis concerns the comparative history of existing domain structures. It did not determine new protein structures experimentally or develop a protein-fold prediction system.

My contribution

I co-designed the study, performed the computational analyses, and co-wrote the paper with Gustavo Caetano-Anollés. This first-author work established a foundation in comparative data representation and model-based biological inference that precedes my regulatory-network and single-cell research.
