POLYAR: predicting polyadenylation sites

The computational problem

Identifying where pre-mRNA is cleaved and polyadenylated helps define transcript boundaries. POLYAR used sequence motifs, their positions, and nucleotide composition to predict human polyadenylation sites. The published study included 28,761 mapped sites and used linear discriminant classifiers for three signal classes.

Method and result

The study reported improved prediction of strong-signal sites relative to its comparator. Weak-signal and signal-less sites remained more difficult, identifying cases where the available sequence features were insufficient. The benchmark therefore described both the method's predictive value and its limitations.

The work illustrates a general computational approach: encode biological knowledge as quantitative features, evaluate performance against an alternative, and examine which classes of examples remain difficult.

My contribution

I shared responsibility for mapped-site data, predictive-feature analysis, initial program development, and search-algorithm design. Malik Nadeem Akhtar performed the final implementation and comparative testing. These contributions are documented in the paper.

This page describes the published 2010 method and benchmark; it does not imply contemporary production or clinical deployment.
