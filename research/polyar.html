BMC Genomics · 2010

POLYAR: predicting RNA-processing sites

Sequence features and statistical classification for human polyadenylation-site prediction.

My role
Equal-contribution methods developer
Published scope
28,761 mapped human poly(A) sites in the published study
Research status
Peer-reviewed publication

The computational problem

Predicting where pre-mRNA is cleaved and polyadenylated helps define transcript boundaries. POLYAR combined sequence motifs, their positions, and nucleotide composition using linear discriminant classifiers for three signal classes.

My contribution

I shared responsibility for mapped-site data, predictive-feature analysis, initial program development, and search-algorithm design. Final implementation and comparative testing were performed by Malik Nadeem Akhtar.

Published result

The study reported improved prediction of strong-signal sites relative to its comparator. Weak-signal and signal-less sites remained difficult, identifying where the available sequence features were insufficient.

Why it matters to my research trajectory

This project established an approach I continue to use: encode biological knowledge in quantitative features, benchmark against alternatives, and investigate failure modes. It documents algorithm and software contributions before my later regulatory-network and single-cell work.

Scope of the method

Published sequence-prediction research. Contribution credit is shared as documented in the paper; the original benchmark is not a claim of contemporary production or clinical deployment.
