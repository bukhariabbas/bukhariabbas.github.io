Proposed independent program

Research directions

My proposed research program asks how regulatory programs create diversity within pediatric tumors and which dependencies warrant experimental testing. It builds on comparative neurogenomics, single-cell regulatory analysis, and current pediatric oncology research. These are future directions, not an established laboratory or a recruitment announcement.

1. Separate developmental programs from tumor adaptation

Compare recurrent malignant-cell programs with relevant developmental references and test their stability in patients held out from discovery. Molecular subtype, proliferation, genomic dosage, and stress provide competing explanations to evaluate. Published RMS studies motivate this direction: Wei et al., Nature Cancer, 2022; Danielli et al., Nature Communications, 2024.

2. Connect programs to testable dependencies

Integrate regulon, functional-dependency, and pharmacologic evidence to nominate regulators and suitable experimental models. Perturbation studies with collaborators would test whether changing a regulator alters the predicted program and relevant phenotype.

3. Evaluate tissue and patient context

Distinguish changes in cell abundance from regulation within cells, test spatial relationships where data permit, and assess outcome associations against established clinical variables. Independent cohorts and event-aware model design would determine which findings generalize.

Research foundations

Initial projects would use public or appropriately authorized datasets, reproducible workflows, and explicitly scoped validation partnerships. A related collaborative direction is brain genomic medicine, building on published neurogenomics contributions while establishing mechanisms within each disease system.
